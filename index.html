Why we eat this way

We’ve seen one too many nature videos. Here are a few. They’re hard to unwatch.

A cow’s udder secretes milk to feed her calf. See inside a real udder. Skip to 18:26 for the close-up.

Bees pass nectar mouth to mouth, then dry it in the comb to make honey for the hive. Watch a bee store nectar.

Eggs are just gross. Inside a hen, the yolk forms first. The white and shell form around it before she lays the egg. Watch how an egg forms with David Attenborough.

Beeswax starts on a bee’s belly. Worker bees secrete tiny flakes of wax there and shape them into honeycomb. On a food label, beeswax can also go by E901. See how it’s made.

Shellac gives some candies their shine. Lac insects leave a resin on tree branches. People collect and refine it to make a glaze you might spot on a box of jelly beans. See how it’s made.

Common questions

So, is this an actual diet?+

We call this way of eating anti-secretionism. Someone who follows it can say, “I’m an anti-secretionist. I eat meat and skip dairy, honey and eggs.”

Does dairy include butter and cheese?+

Yes. Milk, butter, cheese, cream, dairy yogurt, ghee, whey, and other dairy ingredients are off the menu.

What about plant milk and maple syrup?+

They’re welcome. Choose versions without eggs, dairy, or honey. Plant-based yogurt and butter alternatives follow the same rule.

What do I say at a restaurant?+

Show or copy the order note for the kitchen. It names the ingredients so you can use it anywhere.

Can I support this if I eat dairy or honey?+

Absolutely. You can love both and still want your friends to find a meal they can order. Sign the menu petition.
