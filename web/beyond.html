FIELD NOTES

Beyond milk and honey

Meat stays on the menu. We skip eggs, dairy, honey, and ingredients made from other animal secretions. That’s a personal food preference. Here’s how it translates at the grocery store.

Three less obvious skips

Royal jelly
Young worker bees make this food in glands in their heads. It fits the rule even in products that list royal jelly separately from honey. FAO guide to royal jelly
Edible beeswax
Worker bees secrete wax from abdominal glands. Look for “beeswax” or “E901” on coated sweets. Beeswax can also be used on fruit and in chewing gum. WHO entry on beeswax
Shellac
Lac insects secrete a resin that can be processed into a shiny food glaze. “Shellac” or “E904” is a clue on candy packaging. If the label only says “glaze,” ask which kind. Plenty of shiny foods get their finish another way. Where shellac comes from · Codex guide to glazes

The everyday dairy words

Butter, ghee, cheese, yogurt and cream all start with milk. So do whey, casein and milk powder. Lactose-free cow’s milk still starts with cow’s milk. Coconut milk, oat milk and cocoa butter have plant sources. For a finished food, read the whole ingredient list.

Where eggs show up

Watch for whites, yolks, dried egg, and egg wash. Ask about mayonnaise, battered foods, and fresh pasta. For a sandwich, try avocado or plain mustard after checking the ingredients.
