FIELD NOTES

A pantry that makes the second week easier

Start with one meal you already eat twice a week. For a peanut butter and jelly sandwich, the shortest route is a loaf you like, a jar of peanut butter and a jam you’ll actually finish.

Shop the loaf first

Read the loaf’s ingredient list for egg, milk, whey, butter and honey. A bread called “multigrain” tells you something about the grains and very little about what sweetens it. When you find one that works, photograph the front and the ingredients so someone else can pick it up.

Keep the swap specific

For toast, try olive oil and flaky salt. For oatmeal, try maple syrup. For a sharp dressing, shake olive oil, vinegar and plain mustard in a small jar. Add a little maple only if it needs sweetness. A separate substitute for every dairy product is an expensive way to discover you mostly wanted toast.

Give each favorite a backup

If your usual loaf sells out, make a bowl of rice with black beans and roasted vegetables, or keep a second suitable bread in the freezer. A useful pantry gets dinner on the table when the store is out of the one brand you remember.
