NUTRITION GUIDE

Eating well as an anti-secretionist

Even without eggs, dairy, or honey, breakfast can still be more than dry toast. A bowl of oats cooked in fortified soy milk with peanut butter and berries gives you protein, whole grains and fruit. Choose a carton with calcium and vitamin D and you cover some of what fortified dairy milk used to provide.

Build a meal you want to eat again

A rough guide for lunch and dinner is half a plate of vegetables and fruit, a quarter whole grains and a quarter protein. For a burrito bowl, that could be peppers, cabbage and salsa alongside brown rice and black beans or grilled chicken. Avocado and lime finish the bowl. Frozen vegetables and canned beans work here, too. Harvard’s Healthy Eating Plate

Four nutrients worth planning for

Calcium
Choose a calcium-fortified plant milk you enjoy, or tofu made with calcium sulfate. Compare the calcium in milligrams per serving on the Nutrition Facts panel. Kale and bok choy contribute calcium too. NIH calcium guide
Vitamin D
Check the vitamin D line separately on that same carton. Fortification varies by brand. If you skip seafood, look for fortified cereals or mushrooms labeled as UV-exposed as other options. NIH vitamin D guide
Iodine
Dairy, eggs and seafood are common sources. If you eat little of all three, check your salt container for the word “iodized.” Use iodized salt in place of the salt you already cook with, keeping the amount the same. Many sea and kosher salts supply little iodine. NIH iodine guide
Choline
Egg yolks are one convenient source. An egg-free dinner can include chicken or beef with potatoes and broccoli. Soybeans also provide choline, so edamame makes a useful addition to a grain bowl. NIH choline guide

An egg-free day, without seafood

Breakfast • Cook rolled oats in unsweetened fortified soy milk. Swirl in peanut butter and add berries. Banana or cinnamon works if you like it sweeter.

Lunch • Fill corn tortillas with black beans, brown rice, crunchy cabbage, salsa and avocado. Squeeze lime over the top.

Dinner • Roast chicken, broccoli and potatoes with olive oil, garlic and lemon. Make extra chicken for tomorrow’s lunch. For a meatless version, roast cubes of calcium-set tofu alongside the vegetables.

At a breakfast counter • Ask for plain oatmeal cooked in water with fruit and nuts, or a breakfast potato bowl with beans, peppers and avocado. Ask what goes on the griddle and whether the oats or beans are finished with butter.

Two labels to read in the grocery aisle

The ingredient list tells you whether a food fits your preference. The Nutrition Facts panel tells you what it contributes. For a plant milk you’ll drink most days, compare protein, calcium, vitamin D and added sugars at the same serving size. Skipping honey requires no special replacement. Fruit can sweeten breakfast, and maple syrup is an option when you want its flavor.
