No Secretions

This sticker means the food, as offered, meets our ingredient guidelines. We skip dairy, honey, other ingredients made from animal secretions, and eggs.

For a pastry, check the butter in the dough, the filling, the egg wash, and the glaze. If the whole recipe fits, put No Secretions beside the pastry’s name, wherever you already show dietary labels.

What to check before labeling

Start with something people already love. Offer a sandwich on bread baked without milk or eggs, finish a steak with olive oil and salt, or bake a batch of maple-oat cookies without dairy, honey, or eggs. Make that version easy to choose when someone orders online.

Your kitchen or brand checks each dish or product it labels. On ordering sites, we’re asking for an Anti-secretionist friendly filter so diners can find those items before checkout.

  • Check the whole recipe. Meat and plants can fit. Check bread, sauces, cooking fats, garnishes, and glazes for eggs, dairy, honey, royal jelly, beeswax, and shellac, including ingredients derived from them. Confirm anything unclear with the supplier.
  • Describe the exact order. If a dish needs a substitution, name it beside the dish and offer it wherever customers order. For steak that normally comes with butter, apply the label to the olive-oil option and show the swap before checkout.
  • Keep it current. For packaged food, check the specific product and flavor. Review the label again when ingredients, suppliers, or preparation change. Keep allergen and cross-contact guidance available separately.
