What is anti-secretionism?

What do you call a diet that includes meat but skips dairy, honey and eggs?

Anti-secretionism is our community’s name for a way of eating that allows meat and plant foods and excludes dairy, honey, eggs and ingredients derived from animal secretions. A person who follows it can call themselves an anti-secretionist.

An anti-secretionist might order a steak with crisp potatoes cooked in olive oil, or a bowl of black beans, rice, salsa and avocado.

We want more foods made without eggs, milk or honey, from the bread on grocery shelves to the pancakes at the neighborhood diner.

Why are eggs on the list?

We include eggs as a separate house rule. That means checking for egg wash on a roll and egg in mayonnaise as well as skipping an omelet. Other animal-made ingredients we skip include royal jelly, beeswax and shellac. See the ingredient guide.

At the breakfast counter

Try a scramble of crumbled tofu, peppers and onions with a side of hash browns. Ordering at a diner? Ask for potatoes cooked in oil, top them with salsa, and add bacon if you like.

When a recipe calls for milk

Cook oatmeal in oat milk and top it with berries. For creamy mashed potatoes, use plain unsweetened soy milk and olive oil. Save the vanilla-flavored carton for something sweet.

Why put it in the food?

Take a sandwich. Milk can give the roll a softer crumb and help its crust brown. Egg yolk helps the mayonnaise hold oil and water together. Honey brings sweetness and its own flavor to the mustard. Each ingredient does something a cook might want. Does every sandwich need all three? King Arthur’s bread ingredient guide

We’d happily order roast chicken on a flour-water-yeast-and-salt roll, with avocado for creaminess and plain mustard for bite. Our question for bakeries, restaurants and food brands is simple. Would you put a version like that on the menu and label it so we can find it?

Our favorite replacements

Milk for cereal and protein shakes · So Delicious Organic Unsweetened Original Coconutmilk. Chris says he barely tastes the coconut in his cereal or protein shakes.

Honey for drizzling · Pure maple syrup brings a little caramel flavor to oatmeal. For a dressing, whisk it with mustard, cider vinegar and olive oil.

Eggs for a breakfast scramble · Firm tofu crumbles easily into a hot pan. Brown it in olive oil with peppers and onions, then fold it into a warm tortilla with salsa.

What those foods contribute

Milk traditionally supplies protein and calcium, and fortified milk also supplies vitamin D. Plant milks vary. Compare those three numbers per cup on the carton. Fortified soy milk is a good option if you want a nutrient profile closer to dairy milk. FDA guide to milk alternatives

Eggs provide protein and choline. Tofu adds protein to breakfast, and foods such as roasted soybeans and chicken can help supply choline throughout the day. NIH guide to choline

We also skip beeswax and animal saliva-derived ingredients as a personal food preference. For everyday meal planning, our attention goes to the foods that take the place of milk and eggs.
